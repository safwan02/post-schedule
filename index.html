1A 6-year-old drew this Pokémon card. It sold for $156,000

Mon 11/23 · 5 slides

Hang it on the fridge, or sell it for $156,000 🖍️

In 2010, six-year-old Megu Taniguchi colored a printed Zorua and drew a bus and Pikachu around it for a Pokémon card design contest run in Japanese kids' magazines.

The design won the kindergarten category and became a real card, with Megu credited as the artist. Officially, each winner got just 100 copies.

On September 27, 2026, a PSA 10 copy sold on Fanatics Collect for $156,000.

2This Yu-Gi-Oh card was made for a Make-A-Wish kid and sold for $311,211

Mon 11/23 · 7 slides

He kept the best part 🥹

At 14, Tyler Gressle was fighting a rare cancer. He first wished for a Porsche, but was too young to drive. So he asked Make-A-Wish for his own Yu-Gi-Oh! card.

Creator Kazuki Takahashi sketched two designs from Tyler's idea, and one copy was printed in 2005.

Tyler recovered and held on to it for 18 years. In 2023 it sold on eBay for $311,211, money for a family and a business, with a share promised to Make-A-Wish. The three original drawings stayed with Tyler, framed.

3In 1998, Nintendo dropped about 700 plush Pikachus from the sky over Kansas

Tue 11/24 · 6 slides

We used to be a real country 😭

In 1998, Nintendo had to sell Pokémon to a country that barely knew it, so it picked Topeka, Kansas, and Mayor Joan Wagnon proclaimed it ToPikachu for a day.

On August 27, skydivers and about 700 plush Pikachus fell from the sky, and ten Pikachu Beetles left on a national tour. Red and Blue came out a month later. In 2018, Mayor Michelle De La Isla made it ToPikachu for another day.

A sealed 1998 Pokémon Red, graded 9.9 out of 10, sold for $93,750 at Heritage Auctions in May 2025.

4Japan has nearly 500 Pokémon manhole lids. You can't buy a single one

Tue 11/24 · 6 slides

I want to see every single one 😍

Japan started decorating manhole covers in the 1980s to win support for costly new sewers. In 2018, The Pokémon Company joined in and began giving towns free Poké Lids.

The first went to Ibusuki, whose name sounds like "Eevee suki," or "I love Eevee." Kagawa, famous for udon, got Slowpoke, whose Japanese name is Yadon.

Each lid is a PokéStop in Pokémon GO. The Mew lid sits on Chichijima, 24 hours from Tokyo by ferry. There are now nearly 500, none for sale, so to catch them all you have to travel.

5Pikachu was first designed to look like a Japanese rice cake with ears

Wed 11/25 · 5 slides

Now I can't unsee the rice cake 🥹

Before the first Pokémon games came out in 1996, Atsuko Nishida designed Pikachu straight on a computer. The first draft was like a tall daifuku, a filled Japanese rice cake, with ears.

The cheeks came from squirrels, and the “Mouse Pokémon” label followed from the name. Over the years, that round body slimmed down.

An early marketing version of Pikachu's first card in the card game, one of only two certified by grading company CGC, sold privately for $3.3 million in August 2026.

6In 1998, a Japanese airline started turning its planes into Pokémon

Wed 11/25 · 9 slides

Imagine this pulling up to your gate 🤯

In July 1998, ANA launched its first Pokémon Jets. Flyers could mail in boarding-pass stubs for a special Flying Pikachu card, and the jets carried over a million passengers in seven months. A fourth grader's drawing became the next design.

They vanished in 2016 and returned in 2023. For Pokémon's 30th birthday, Red and Green jets are flying, and Blue joins December 12, 2026.

In May 2026, a 1999 Flying Pikachu card, graded PSA 10, sold on eBay for $7,525.

7Pikachu has flown over New York every Thanksgiving since 2001

Thu 11/26 · Thanksgiving · 6 slides

Thanksgiving isn't Thanksgiving without him 🦃

In 2001, Pikachu became the first Pokémon to fly in the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade. It got a new balloon and a giant Poké Ball in 2006, and a holiday makeover with a scarf and a snowman Pikachu in 2014.

8This book is full of typos. It sold for $14.2 million

Thu 11/26 · Thanksgiving · 7 slides

Typos and all 🦃

In 1640, Puritans printed a book of psalms in Cambridge, Mass. The man running the press was a locksmith, not a printer, so it's full of blurry type and mistakes. It's also the first book printed in what's now the United States.

About 1,700 were made. Only 11 survive.

13 years ago today, a Boston church sold one at Sotheby's for $14,165,000, then a record for any printed book.

9Japanese kids had to pass a Pokémon exam to earn a card that sold for $210,800

Fri 11/27 · 8 slides

Finally, a test worth studying for 😅

In 1998, Shogakukan's school-grade magazines opened Tamamushi University, a pretend Pokémon college kids attended by mail.

The last test, the Hyper Professor exam, had 1,000 places across six grades. In sixth grade alone, 21,684 kids entered and 198 passed. Passing earned a diploma and this Magikarp, which knows Dragon Rage.

In February 2026, a top-graded copy of that prize sold at Goldin for $210,800.

10A spoon bender sued Nintendo. Kadabra cards vanished for 20 years. Then one sold for $16,000

Fri 11/27 · 10 slides

All of this over a spoon 😭

In 2000, Uri Geller sued Nintendo over Kadabra, whose Japanese name, Yungerer, resembles his own and who carries his signature spoon.

Geller lost, but Kadabra still disappeared from new cards after 2003. In 2008, the Pokémon cartoon's director reportedly said the dispute was still blocking it.

In 2020, Geller said he was "truly sorry" and was "releasing the ban." In 2023, after 20 years, Kadabra returned in the Pokémon 151 set.

In August 2026, a PSA 10 of a shiny 2002 Kadabra, printed just before the gap, sold on eBay for $16,000.

11This Game Boy burned in the Gulf War and still ran Tetris

Sat 11/28 · 6 slides

They don't make them like this anymore 😅

In 1991, Gulf War nurse Stephan Scoggins mailed the burned remains of his Game Boy to Nintendo and asked for a replacement.

Nintendo didn't expect it to work. It put in Tetris and the Game Boy switched on. Scoggins got a new one, and the burned one went on display in Nintendo's New York store.

Its sign blamed a bombing (Scoggins later said it was a tent fire) and ended with three words: "It still works!" It came off display in 2023.

12Kids were promised this action figure for free. 45 years later, one sold for $1.34 million

Sat 11/28 · 7 slides

They got a note instead of a rocket 😭

In 1979, Kenner offered a free Boba Fett by mail to kids who bought four Star Wars figures. The packages promised a rocket-firing backpack.

Testing went badly and the rockets were a choking worry, so the launcher was dropped. Kids got a Boba with the rocket fixed in place, plus a note offering a swap.

About 100 working test figures had been made, and some went home with Kenner staff. A painted one, one of three known, sold at Goldin in August 2024 for $1,342,000.

13He paid $75 for old boxes and found a $360,000 Nintendo PlayStation

Sun 11/29 · 9 slides

Go check your old boxes right now 👀

Nintendo and Sony once teamed up on a console that played cartridges and CDs. Nintendo backed out, about 200 test units never reached stores, and Sony launched its own PlayStation in 1994.

One ended up in boxes Terry Diebold bought for $75 at a bankrupt company's auction. It sat in his attic until his son Dan shared it in 2015.

Terry said they turned down $1.2 million. In 2020 it sold at Heritage for $360,000, including the buyer's premium.

14Only 2 pink ones are known. He found both. One is valued at $150,000

Sun 11/29 · 6 slides

He made the calls nobody else did 📞

The first Volkswagen Beach Bomb from Hot Wheels, made in 1969, had surfboards out the back. It was too skinny, tipped over, and was redesigned before it reached stores.

A few dozen test cars survived, mostly with company workers, and only two pink ones are known. Collector Bruce Pascal tracked down both by 2001.

He sold one and kept the other. He has estimated it at $150,000, which is his valuation and not a sale.

15This free Pikachu card caused chaos at the Van Gogh Museum. One later sold for $14,500

Mon 11/30 · 7 slides

Vincent would not believe the fuss 🎨

In 2023, the Van Gogh Museum celebrated its 50th birthday with six Pokémon remakes of his paintings and a free Pikachu card for finishing an art hunt.

Crowds packed in, sellers asked over $300, and that October the museum stopped giving out the card for safety.

More went out online that fall and in Dutch shops in 2024. Still, in September 2026, a copy graded 10 by Beckett sold on eBay for $14,500.

16Atari dumped this game in a landfill. 31 years later, one sold for $1,537

Mon 11/30 · 9 slides

One man's trash, literally 👽

In 1982, Atari gave one programmer five weeks to make an E.T. game. It flopped, and in 1983 Atari dumped truckloads of games and gear in a New Mexico landfill.

In 2014, a documentary crew dug up about 1,300 cartridges. One E.T. went to the Smithsonian.

On eBay, 881 games from that garbage raised $107,930. The priciest, a boxed E.T., brought $1,537.
